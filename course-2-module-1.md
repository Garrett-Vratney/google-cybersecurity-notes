### Definition
- Asset security involves managing the cybersecurity process of organizational assets, including store, maitence, retentenion, and destruction of physical and virtual data.

## Domain Five: Identity and Access Management

### Definition
-  This domain focuses on keeping data secure, this is done by ensuring user identities are trusted and authenticated and that access to physical and logical assets is authorized.
-  IAM domain uses what is referred to as the principle of least privledge, the principle's concept of granting only minimal access and authorization required to complete a task.

#### Example
A cybersecurity analyst might be asked to ensure that customer service representative can only view the private data of a customer, such as their phone number, while working to resolve the customer's issue, then remove access when the issue is resolved.

## Threat

### Definition
Any circumstance or event that can negatively impact assets

A social engineering attack.

## Social Engineering

### Definition
A manipulation technique that exploits human error to gain private information, access, or valuables.

### Example
A threat actor manipulates a CEO of an organization by posing as a keen investor, attempting to gain access to private information or valuables.

## Risk

### Definiton
Anything that can impact the confidentiality, integirty, or avaiblity of an asset.

#### Example
Think of a risk as the liklihood of a threat occuring; an example of a risk to an organization might be the lack of backup protocols for making sure its stored information can be recovered in the event of an accident or security incident.

---

## Low Risk Asset

### Definiton
Information that would not harm the organization's reputation or ongoing operations, and would not cause financial damage if compromised

#### Example
Public information

## Medium Risk Asset

### Definiton 
Information that's not avaialble to the public and may cause some damage to the organization's finance, reputation, or ongoing operations.

#### Example
An example of a medium risk asset would be the early release of companies quarterly earnings, thus impacting its stock and valuation.

## High Risk Asset

### Definition
Information protected by regulations or laws, which if compromised would have a severe negative impact on an organizations finances, ongoing operations and reputation.

#### Example
Leaked assets which include SPII, PII, or intellectual property.

# NIST Risk Management Framework

## Seven steps for the framework

### Frameworks are used by security professionals to manage risks, threats and vulnerabilities.

---
## RMF Step 1: Prepare

### Definition
Activities that are necessary to manage security and privacy risks before a breach occurs

#### Example
As an entry level analyst you will likely use this step to monitor for risks and identify controls which can be used to mitigate risks.

## RMF Step 2: Categorize

### Defintion
Used to develop risk management processes and tasks.

#### Example
As an entry level anaylsts you will be needed to use those processes and develop tasks by thinking about how the confidentiality, integirty, and avaialbity of the systems and information can be impacted by risk.

## RMF Step 3: Select

### Definition
Choose, customize, and capture documentation of the controls that protect an organization

### Example
Keeping a playbook up to date, helping to manage other documentation which helps you and your team to address issues more efficiently

## RMF Step 4: Implement

### Definition
Implement security and privacy plans for your organization

#### Example
If you notice a pattern of employees consistently needing password resets, implementing a change to password requirements may help the issue.

## RMF Step 5: Assess

### Definition
Determine if established controls are implemented correctly

### Example
Analysts identify potential weakenesses and determine whether the organizations tools, procedures, controls, and protocols whould be changed to better manage potential risks.

## RMF Step 6: Authorize

### Definition
Authorize means being accountable for the security and privacy risks that may exist in an organization

#### Example
As an anaylst this step could mean; generating reports, developing plans of action, and establishing project milestones which are aligned with the organizations security goals.

## RMF Step 7: Monitory

### Definition
Be aware of how systems are operating

#### Example
Assessing and maintaing techical operations are tasks that analysts complete daily, maintaining a low level of risk for an organization is knowing how the current systems work in supporting the organizations security goals.

# Manage common threats, risks, and vulnerabilities

## Risk management

### Definition
A primary goal of organization to protect assets

#### Common strategies used to manage risks
- Acceptance: Accepting a risk to avoid disrupting business continuity
- Avoidance: Creating a plan to avoid the risk altogether
- Transference: Transferring risk to a third party to manage
- Mitigation: Lessening the impact of a known risk

---

## Common threats include
- Insider threats: Staff members or vendors abuse their authroized access to obtain data which may harm an organization
- Advanced persistent threats (APTS): A threat actor maintains unauthorized access to a system for an extended period of time.

## Different factors that can affect the liklihood of a risk to an organization
- External Risk: Anything outside the organization that has the potential to hard organizational assets, threat actors attempting to gain access to PII
- Internal Risk: A current or former employee, vendor, or trusted partner who poses a security risk
- Legacy Systems: Old systems may need a patch, can still impact assests such as old workstations or old mainframe systems.
- Multiparty Risk: Outsourcing work to third-party vendors can give them access to intellectual property such as trade secrets, software designs, etc.
- Software Compliance/liscensing: Software that is not updated or in compliance, or patches that are not installed in a timely manner.

## Vulnerabilities Include
- Proxylogon: A pre-authenticated vulnerability that affects the Microsoft Exchange Server. This menas a threat actor can complete a user authentication process to deploy malicious code from a remote location.
- ZeroLogon: A vulnerability in Microsoft's Netlogon authentication protocol. An authentication protocol is a way to verify a persons identity. Netlogon is a service that ensures a user identity before allowing access to a websites location.
- Log4Shell: Allow attackers to run java code on someone else's computer or leak sensitive information. It does this by enabling a remote attacker to take control of devices connected to the internet and run malicious code.
- Petit4Potam: Affects Windows New Technology Local Area (LAN) manager (NTLM). It is a theft technique that allows a LAN-Based attacker to iniate an authentication request.
- Security logging and monitoring failures: Insufficient logging and monitoring capabilities that result in attackers exploiting vulnerabilities without the organization knowing it.
- Server-side request forgery: Allows attackers to manipulate a server-side application into accessing and updating backend resources. It can also allow threat actors to steal data.
